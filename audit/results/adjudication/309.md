## Instrumented findings (run against frozen `AAPL_5m.pkl` via the tape_exec stack)

**(a) ref2 has a genuine bookkeeping defect — stale/orphaned stop orders.** Ref2 places the stop inside `notify_order` and cancels it in `next()` on a z-exit. When entry-fill and z-exit signal land on the same bar, the stop order is still in broker **'Submitted'** state when `self.cancel()` runs; backtrader's `BackBroker.cancel` silently no-ops on non-pending orders (instrumentation: cancel attempt 2026-04-16T13:30, status stays `Submitted`). The orphaned stop later fills against a *different* trade: 2026-04-17T15:30 BUY at 272.13598… = 1.02 × 266.79998… (the 2026-04-16 13:30 entry), covering the *current* short (entered 268.394) at a ~1.4% adverse move — neither its 2% stop (273.762) nor a z-exit. Each stale fill orphans the then-tracked stop in turn (its cancel path never runs), cascading: **6 stale stop fills total**, which is what makes ref2's tape 364 vs 355 fills and shifts subsequent entries.

**(b) Genuine 2% stop-outs fire on this data, and the two faithful stop designs give them different timestamps.** Instrumented ref0: **11 stop exits** (167 z-exits). Example, the long entered 2026-04-21 at 272.65: refs 0/1 detect low ≤ 267.197 at the 15:45 bar and the market `self.close()` fills at the **15:50** open (267.24); ref2's *correctly-tracked, non-stale* stop for the same trade fills **intrabar at 15:45** at the stop price 267.197. Same economic event, different `(timestamp, side)` key — and this instance survives any repair of defect (a). With 11 such events plus their re-entry cascades, a leak-free ref2 still cannot reproduce the 355-fill tape.

## Classification reasoning (D4)

- Defect (a) *is* contradicted by explicit spec text — "Include a 2% hard stop-loss from entry on every trade": six trades were exited by stops priced 2% from *other* trades' entries, so those trades did not carry a 2%-from-entry stop. That makes it an implementation error eligible for the one repair round, listed as a repair candidate.
- But the repair is **moot for classification**: finding (b) shows the surviving divergence between a repaired ref2 and refs 0/1 traces to the unpinned choice of stop mechanics (intrabar native stop fill at the stop price vs bar-close breach detection exiting at next open). Neither design is contradicted by any spec sentence — if anything a resting stop order is the more literal reading of "hard stop-loss," while `self.close()` is closer to the D4a exit default; the spec pins the level, not the mechanism. Per D4, divergence traceable to an unpinned spec choice ⇒ **INDETERMINATE**.
- Note also that the only exact convergence (ref0 = ref1) is same-model (opus×2); the cross-model pair diverged, which D4a treats as the weaker form of evidence.

**Ambiguity class:** `unpinned-exit` — the stop-loss is an exit rule whose trigger/execution semantics ("2% hard stop-loss from entry") are unpinned. The screen's second predicted class (`unpinned-entry-trigger`, level-vs-cross on "drops below -2") did not manifest: all three refs used identical level checks.

No canonical tape is written for this task.
