## Task summary

ATR(14)/EMA(50) trend-following pullback strategy with scaling-in: buy when price
is above EMA(50) and pulls back 1.5·ATR(14) "from a recent high"; add on each
further 1.5·ATR pullback "from the next high" up to 4 consecutive entries; exit
the whole position at +2.5·ATR take-profit "from the entry point" or on a close
below EMA(50); per-position stop at entry − 1.5·ATR; each entry sized at 1% of
capital.

## Where and why the tapes diverge (each mapped to quoted spec text)

### 1. `unpinned-entry-trigger` — "a recent high" / "the next high" undefined

Spec: entry 2 — *"Open a long position when the price makes a pullback
(correction) equal to 1.5 ATR(14) from a recent high"*; entry 3 — *"If after
entering the position the price continues to fall and reaches a new pullback
level of 1.5 ATR(14) from the next high, open an additional position (scaling
in)."*

Neither "a recent high" nor "the next high" is given a lookback window, swing
definition, or ATR anchor bar. All three refs happened to converge on a 20-bar
rolling high for the *initial* trigger (a declared assumption in each), but the
**scale-in reference** diverges three ways:

- **ref0:** freezes the initial reference high AND the ATR at first entry;
  additional entry k triggers at `ref_high − k·1.5·ATR_frozen` (a fixed ladder).
- **ref1:** "next high" = highest high since the previous entry (reset after
  each fill), with the *current* bar's ATR.
- **ref2:** "next high" = running max high since the last fill, seeded with the
  fill bar's high, current ATR.

This (together with #3 below) is what splits ref0 from ref1 at fill idx 11
(2020-09-09), where ref0 issues a scale-in BUY and ref1 a stop SELL on the same
bar. Note ref0's ladder never re-measures from any "next" high; this was
considered as a possible explicit-text violation ("from the next high"), but the
phrase has no determinate referent — in the spec's own scenario ("the price
continues to fall") no new higher high forms, so "the next high" cannot be read
literally and every ref had to invent a definition. An undefined phrase cannot
unambiguously contradict an implementation, so no repair is warranted.

### 2. `unpinned-exit` — trigger price, stop scope, and same-bar priority

Spec exit 1: *"Close the entire position upon reaching a take-profit of 2.5
ATR(14) from the entry point."* — "upon reaching" does not say whether the level
is detected on the daily close or intrabar. refs 0/1 require `close >= tp`;
ref2 fires on `high >= tp`. This alone produces the first tape split: ref2
sells on 2020-07-08 (bar high touched TP on 07-07), refs 0/1 on 2020-07-09
(close crossed a day later). Only the EMA exit is explicitly close-based
(*"if the price closes below EMA(50)"*); TP and stop triggers are unpinned.

Spec risk rule 3: *"Set a stop-loss for each position at 1.5 ATR(14) below the
entry point. If the stop-loss is triggered, the position is forcibly closed."*
— "for each position" implies per-entry stops, but "the position is forcibly
closed" has an ambiguous antecedent (that individual position vs the aggregate
position). refs 0/1 partial-close only the breached lot; ref2 closes the entire
accumulated position (also citing backtrader's netted-position model, which has
no native per-lot close). "Take-profit ... from the entry point" (singular) with
up to 4 entries is likewise unpinned; all three anchored to the first entry, so
this particular gap did not separate the tapes, but it is declared in all three
assumption lists.

Same-bar priority is nowhere specified and differs three ways: ref0 evaluates
EMA-break → TP → scale-in → stops; ref1 evaluates TP → EMA-break → stops →
scale-in; ref2 evaluates stops → EMA-break → TP → scale-in. ref0's own
assumption notes the scale-in level numerically coincides with the prior entry's
stop, so this ordering is behaviorally decisive (it is the other half of the
2020-09-09 BUY-vs-SELL split between ref0 and ref1).

### 3. `unpinned-sizing` — 1% of capital vs whole shares under the AAPL/$10,000 binding

Spec: *"The size of each individual position must be 1% of the total trading
capital."* 1% of the harness's $10,000 is ~$100, which is **less than one AAPL
share** for most of the 2020–2025 window. The spec does not say whether
fractional shares are permitted:

- **refs 0/1:** fractional notional sizing `0.01·value/close` (exact 1%),
  declared as required "to honor the 1%-per-position rule".
- **ref2:** whole shares `int(0.01·value/close)` → 0 or 1 share, declared
  ("the small capital base relative to AAPL price produces minimal share
  counts").

Consequence: ref2's tape has 9 fills and terminates on 2020-08-03 — once AAPL
closes above ~$100 the floor yields size 0 and no entry is ever placed again.
Neither rounding convention contradicts the quoted sentence; the D4a default
("plain `self.buy()` when sizing is unstated") does not apply because sizing IS
stated, just not pinned to a share-rounding rule under this binding. This is an
unpinned choice amplified by the bench's D2 binding (generic-ETF prose executed
on AAPL with $10,000 cash).
