## Where the codes differ

All three implement the *pinned* rules identically:

- **Volume confirmation** — all use `SMA(volume, 50)` and require breakout-bar volume strictly above it, per
  "Volume on the breakout candle must be above the average volume over the last 50 candles".
- **Breakout trigger price** — all require the bar's **close** above the neckline, jointly satisfying the
  mandatory "Price must break upward above the resistance level" and the soft "**Preferably**, the closing
  price of the breakout candle should be above the resistance level".
- **Exit** — all close the position when the daily close drops below the stored handle low, per "Close the
  position when price drops below the lowest point of the handle of the pattern".
- **Neckline** — all define resistance = max(left rim, right rim), per "the upper boundary of the cup (the
  pattern neckline)".
- **Sizing skeleton** — all compute `size = risk_amount / (entry − handle_low)`, per "Position size must be
  calculated based on the stop-loss".
- D4a defaults (long-only, single position, no pyramiding, pending-order guard) — all comply.

They differ only in the **pattern detector**, which the spec never quantifies:

| aspect | ref0 | ref1 | ref2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| formation window | rolling 400-bar scan, left rim forced ≥250 bars back | fixed 220-bar cup + 30-bar handle | fixed 200-bar cup + 50-bar handle |
| rim location | argmax high in free early/late regions | max high of outer 20% cup edges | max high of first/last third |
| rim symmetry tol | 10% | 10% of resistance | 5% |
| cup depth bounds | 10–60% of rim | ≥10% of resistance | none (bottom < both rims) |
| U-vs-V check | each side ≥20% of cup width | bottom in central 25–75% band | thirds structure implicit |
| handle length | free, 5–60 bars | fixed 30 bars | fixed 50 bars |
| handle depth cap | ≤1/3 of cup height | ≤50% of cup depth | ≤30% of cup height |
| handle ceiling | (implicit via rim scan) | strictly below resistance | ≤ neckline × 1.02 |
| fresh-cross required | no | yes (`close[-1] <= resistance`) | no |
| risk per trade | 2% | 1% | 1% |

Ref0's permissive rolling scan finds three patterns (2023–2025); ref1's rigid 220+30 split with a
fresh-cross condition finds exactly one (2025-08-14); ref2's thirds scan with a 5% symmetry tolerance never
fires on AAPL 1d. Each detector is a defensible reading of the same qualitative text.

## Why: quoted unpinned spec text

1. **`unpinned-entry-trigger`** — the entry precondition is a pattern the spec defines only qualitatively:
   "The cup is a U-shaped formation, and the handle is a consolidation or a small decline after the bottom of
   the cup" and "Use only price data (OHLC) to identify the pattern." No tolerances for cup depth, rim
   symmetry, handle depth, or pivot detection are given. This single gap explains the entire primary-key
   divergence (three detectors → three different breakout-date sets, including "never").
2. **`unpinned-indicator-params`** — the formation budget is pinned only as a minimum: "The formation time of
   the 'Cup and Handle' pattern must be at least 1 year (for example, on a daily chart this would be about
   250 candles)" — nothing splits those ~250 bars between cup and handle (400-window vs 220+30 vs 200+50).
   The spec also *explicitly* un-pins the volume period: "over the last 50 candles **(or another reasonable
   period to determine average volume)**" — all three happened to choose 50, so this particular gap did not
   drive the observed divergence, but it is spec-level un-pinning by the task's own text.
3. **`unpinned-sizing`** — "Risk per trade must not exceed 1-2% of total capital" is a range, not a value:
   ref0 chose 2%, refs 1–2 chose 1%. This shows up in the fill sizes (secondary key, D3) — e.g. ref0's sizes
   23/33/12 vs ref1's 3 — though the primary-key divergence is already fully explained by (1)–(2).

Latent but not an observed driver: "Close the position when price drops below the lowest point of the handle"
does not pin close-based vs intrabar-low triggering (`unpinned-exit` in the static screen); all three refs
coincidentally chose close-based, so it produced no divergence here and is not counted.

Note on "timeframe of 1 year or higher": the spec self-resolves this to "~250 candles on a daily chart", and
all three refs implemented the ≥250-daily-bar reading on the bound AAPL 1d feed, so no `timeframe-mismatch`
class applies. Ref2 flagged the phrase as an assumption but converged on the same reading.
