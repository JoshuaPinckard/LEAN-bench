## Convergence result

3/3 refs ran successfully; 3 distinct primary-key tapes (16, 8, and 10 fills). Convergence
summary proposal: DIVERGED. All three share the same warm-up (strategy minperiod 51 via
`Highest(high(-1), 50)`), the same breakout definition (close > highest HIGH of the 50 bars
strictly prior — all three independently converged despite "highest price" being ambiguous),
the same retest test (`low <= EMA10 <= close`), and the same volume confirmation
(`volume > SMA50(volume)`). The divergence is entirely downstream of three genuinely
unpinned spec choices, each of which maps to a concrete tape difference.

## Divergence locus 1 — overlapping breakouts vs the 5-day retest window (`unpinned-entry-trigger`)

Spec: *"Retest: Within the next 5 trading days after the breakout, price must pull back to
the 10-day EMA, but must not close below it."* The spec never says what "the breakout"
means when a new breakout close occurs while a retest window is already open (routine in a
sustained rally, where nearly every day closes above the prior 50-day high).

- **ref0 (opus):** restarts the 5-day window on every fresh breakout close (declared
  assumption "breakout window re-trigger"). Effect: during the July-2020 AAPL rally the
  window stays continuously armed, so the first EMA pullback produces **BUY 2020-07-15** —
  a trade absent from both other tapes.
- **ref1 (opus) and ref2 (sonnet):** anchor the window to the first breakout and ignore new
  breakout signals while watching (ref1: "window tied to a single breakout"; ref2:
  "new breakout signals are ignored while an active 5-day retest watch is open"). Their
  first entry is 2020-12-22.

Neither reading contradicts any explicit sentence; the spec is simply silent on overlap.

## Divergence locus 2 — fixed vs dynamic stop, stop reference bar, and take-profit trigger (`unpinned-exit`)

The spec's two exit statements contradict each other:

- Exit conditions: *"Stop-loss: Close the position if price closes below the 10-day EMA."*
  (unqualified → a dynamic/trailing EMA stop)
- Risk management: *"The stop-loss should be set at the level of the 10-day EMA at the time
  of entry into the position."* (→ a fixed price stop)

All three refs flagged this tension in ASSUMPTIONS and resolved it differently:

- **ref2:** dynamic trailing stop (`close < current EMA10`). On the shared 2020-12-22 entry
  it exits **SELL 2021-01-05**, then churns an extra round trip (BUY 2021-01-29 /
  SELL 2021-02-01) that no other ref has.
- **ref0 and ref1:** fixed stop at the entry-time EMA — but they disagree on what "at the
  time of entry" means. ref0 captures the EMA on the **fill bar** (in `notify_order`),
  ref1 on the **signal bar**. In the rising Dec-2020 market ref0's stop is one bar higher,
  so it fires earlier: ref0 **SELL 2021-01-07** vs ref1 holding on.
- Take-profit: *"Close the position upon reaching a take-profit of 10% from the entry
  price."* "Reaching" is not pinned to close or intrabar. ref0/ref2 use
  `close >= entry*1.10`; ref1 uses intrabar `high >= entry*1.10`, which is why ref1's
  first exit is **SELL 2021-01-26** (high touched +10% on 2021-01-25) rather than a stop.

Each reading is a defensible literal reading of one of the quoted sentences; because the
sentences conflict, no ref can be charged with violating explicit text.

## Divergence locus 3 — fractional vs whole shares under "2% of capital" (`unpinned-sizing`)

Spec: *"Position size should be 2% of total trading capital per trade."* 2% of ~$10,000 is
~$200. The spec does not say whether fractional shares are permitted.

- **ref0/ref1:** fractional shares (`size = 0.02*value/close`, ~0.8–2.1 shares).
- **ref2:** whole shares via `int(...)`, with a `size > 0` guard.

By Dec 2024, AAPL closes near $250 > the $200 allocation, so ref2's floor yields **0
shares** and it silently skips the entry (verified numerically: ref2's portfolio value
after its last 2023 trade is ~$10,006.5; `int(0.02*10006.5/246..254) = 0`). ref0 and ref1
both take the trade: **BUY 2024-12-20 / SELL 2025-01-03**, absent from ref2's tape. This is
a primary-key divergence caused purely by sizing granularity, which the spec leaves open.
(Note this defeats the usual "sizing never affects the (timestamp, side) key" heuristic.)

## Non-divergence-driving spec defects (recorded, not classified as drivers)

- *"The strategy should be applied only to technology sector stocks, especially in the
  cybersecurity and artificial intelligence sub-industries"* — no sector metadata exists on
  the single bound AAPL feed (`external-data-required`). All three refs independently
  treated it as satisfied/pass-through, so it caused no divergence.
- *"Stocks must have average volatility (ATR over 14 days) above the market average"* — a
  cross-sectional comparison is impossible with one feed (`multi-asset-required`). ref0/ref1
  instantiate ATR(14) without gating (no tape effect: minperiod 15 < 51); ref2 omits it.
  Converged behavior, no divergence.

## Repair analysis

No repair candidates. Every behavioral difference traces to a spec sentence that is either
absent (overlapping breakouts, fractional shares, "reaching" semantics, "time of entry"
bar) or internally contradictory (fixed-at-entry vs closes-below-EMA stop). No ref's code
contradicts an explicit, unambiguous spec sentence. Repairing any ref would require
choosing a spec interpretation, which is exactly what INDETERMINATE records.

**Classification:** INDETERMINATE (matches the static screen's prediction, which also
flagged `unpinned-exit` and the infeasible filters; the screen missed that sizing would
reach the primary key via integer flooring).
